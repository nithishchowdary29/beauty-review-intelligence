Week 1 Problem Definition

This Week 1 report defines the business problem of identifying potentially dissatisfied feedback in beauty product reviews. It is part of a consistent six week data science project titled Beauty Review Intelligence, prepared for the Beauty and Wellness internship theme. The overall project examines how text analysis could help a customer experience team prioritize comments for human review. Its positive class is a product review with a rating of one or two, while ratings from three to five form the other class. The rating is used only to define the label and is never supplied as a predictor.

The report explains the decision owner, prediction moment, target label, research questions, hypotheses, variables and business success criteria. It also presents industry context, academic dataset references, and the distinction between rating based filtering and classification of unrated feedback. The discussion links technical choices to the actual business decision, including the limitations of using star ratings as a proxy for dissatisfaction and the need to inspect original feedback before taking action. The methods and outputs are organized so that an evaluator can trace this stage to the preceding and following weekly tasks.

The practical project uses clearly labelled synthetic records generated by a reproducible Python script. No actual customer dataset was downloaded or analyzed, and the report does not claim real brand performance, deployment, financial benefit or certified working hours. Public sources support the research context and proposed future sourcing strategy. Any numerical experiment results refer only to generated data, while unexecuted tests and real world extensions are identified as proposals.

Supporting repository files include the executable pipeline, dependency versions, data provenance labels, cleaning audit, partition records, model metrics and chart outputs. These materials make the completed demonstration inspectable and repeatable. The report provides a foundation for a later study using appropriately sourced real reviews and independent human validation rather than presenting simulated evidence as a finished production system.
